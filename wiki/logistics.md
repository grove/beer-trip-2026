# November logistics

[Home](../README.md) · [Five-day plan](five-day-plan.md) · [November events](november-events.md)

**Planning assumptions:** two adults; five usable days; public transport and walking; no exact dates, arrival airport, accommodation or dietary requirements supplied. The suggested routes are editorial planning blocks rather than booked itineraries. All times in this wiki are local UK time. Research checked 28 October 2026.

## Choose two bases

For a pub-focused trip, my default is two nights in central Manchester followed by two nights in central Liverpool. This gives both cities proper evenings and avoids repeatedly commuting after a long session. Reverse it if your flights favour the other direction. A single base can work, but make the last train a deliberate constraint.

In Manchester, consider accommodation that makes the Northern Quarter/Ancoats and the central stations convenient. In Liverpool, the centre between the station district, Dale Street and the Georgian Quarter works well for the main walking clusters. These are location suggestions, not hotel recommendations or claims about price.

Manchester–Liverpool is a straightforward rail corridor, but departure station and journey length depend on the service: do not treat Piccadilly, Oxford Road and Victoria as interchangeable. National Rail lists direct Manchester services to Liverpool Lime Street and a fastest example of 38 minutes; for planning, allow extra time for walking to the correct station, waiting, luggage and disruption rather than building a pub schedule around the fastest train. [National Rail: Liverpool](https://www.nationalrail.co.uk/discover-by-train/great-british-destinations/visit-liverpool-by-train/).

## Local transport

### Manchester: Bee Network buses and Metrolink

The official contactless rules distinguish the modes: tap on when boarding a bus; on a tram, tap in before boarding and tap out at the final stop. Use the same physical card or the same device throughout a journey and for capping. Each adult needs their own payment method. A phone and the bank card behind it are treated separately. Check the current zones and caps on the [Bee Network contactless page](https://tfgm.com/ways-to-pay/contactless).

Do not assume a bus/tram cap covers a National Rail trip to Stockport, Wigan, Stalybridge or Macclesfield. Buy/check the appropriate rail ticket for the chosen journey. Altrincham, Bury and Rochdale are possible tram corridors; use the planner to compare the relevant stop with the actual pub address rather than assuming every pub in the town is by the station.

### Liverpool: Merseyrail and cross-river trips

Merseyrail's network includes the Southport corridor and Wirral routes towards Chester, West Kirby and New Brighton. See its [network map](https://merseyrail.org/journey-planning/plan-your-journey/network-map/) alongside the disruption information above.

The [Day Saver page](https://www.merseyrail.org/tickets-passes/daily-travel/day-saver/) describes off-peak unlimited travel within the applicable area(s), excluding the weekday morning peak. It explicitly excludes the City Line. At research time the same page says Tap & Go is unavailable at Chester; for a Chester outing, obtain a valid ticket rather than assuming contactless will work there. Prices, products and coverage should be checked for your actual journey.

Wirral is a region, not one walkable pub cluster. Birkenhead, New Brighton, Hoylake/West Kirby and Heswall/Raby require different plans. Some rural pubs need a bus or taxi beyond the rail leg. Select a coherent cluster from the [Liverpool and Wirral directory](areas/liverpool.md).

## Keep the day enjoyable

Choose three or four anchor venues plus optional nearby alternatives. A half pint or shared tasting where offered makes more sense than ordering a full pint simply to tick a pub. Leave room for a meal and for staying longer when a place is excellent. Use the wiki to make good substitutions, not to race through its entire catalogue.

Check kitchen service separately from pub opening. Reserve a meal where the operator recommends it; a table booking is not automatically needed for a casual drink. Traditional pubs and small micropubs may have limited seating; do not assume level access, accessible toilets or a quiet room without checking the venue.

Put outdoor/rural legs and long station walks earlier in the day, and keep an indoor alternative for wet weather. If your dates overlap home football fixtures or major events, check the relevant venues and travel plans before committing to a tight schedule. No fixture or weather forecast is assumed here.

## The useful pre-trip check

For each day's anchors, verify the opening day, first service, kitchen window and whether any private event affects access. For a cider detour, ask which producer and product are actually available, including bottles and perry. For a brewery or festival, confirm whether ordinary walk-in service is available or a specific session ticket is required. Save the return journey and a nearby replacement pub before setting out.
