## What the classification means

CAMRA's current definition centres on fermentation from fresh-pressed apple or pear juice, without concentrate or the concentration-and-dilution process described as chaptalisation in its guidance. Whether the product is bottled, canned, boxed, on a handpump or on a keg line does not settle the question. [CAMRA producer guidance](https://camra.org.uk/producers).

Cloudiness is not proof. Neither is a vague "traditional" label or an unqualified "100% juice" claim. Pasteurisation alone does not disqualify a cider under current CAMRA guidance, although a live/unpasteurised cider is a further distinction you may care about. Check the actual product: a producer can make both qualifying and non-qualifying ranges, and a missing producer-list entry is not proof that every product fails. [CAMRA cider FAQ](https://camra.org.uk/promotional-campaigns/promoting-real-cider-and-perry/faqs).

## At the bar

Ask what is currently available from fresh-pressed juice, who made it, and whether there is perry as well as cider. Ask about bottles as well as draught: a serious selection can be in the fridge or cellar. If live fermentation matters to you, ask that separately from whether it meets the real-cider definition. A cider award can identify a promising venue, but the dated evidence and today's product list decide the visit.
