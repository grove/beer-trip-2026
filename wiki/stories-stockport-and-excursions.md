## The Arden Arms: an eighteenth-century cookery author is part of this pub's family tree

The **[Arden Arms](venues/arden-arms-stockport.md)** was built in **1815**, then owned by the **Raffald family**. First landlord **George Raffald** was a nephew of **Elizabeth Raffald**, whose enormously influential 18th-century cookery writing and pioneering Manchester trade directory make her a surprising connection to a pint in Stockport. The pub passed to **Robinsons around 1889**. This is a much more interesting backstory than an unsourced claim that the pub is simply "old". [Historic England's Stockport historic-pub walk](https://historicengland.org.uk/campaigns/visit/walking-tours/historic-pub-walks-north-west-england/stockport/).

**Treasure hunt:** note the numbered doors, once a licensing requirement; the **bell pushes in the Vault's tables**, which customers once used to summon service; and the handpumps unusually fixed to the **back bar**, rather than the counter. Then ask a member of staff about the **"Select" snug**: it is an extremely rare tiny room reached **through the staff servery**, *not* somewhere visitors should walk uninvited. Its exclusivity once probably suited the landlord's close acquaintances. The interior, not a guaranteed particular cask, is the reason to get here in time for the documented lunch service. [Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/campaigns/visit/walking-tours/historic-pub-walks-north-west-england/stockport/) · [Robinsons pub page](https://www.robinsonsbrewery.com/pubs/arden-arms-stockport/).

## Swan with Two Necks: a century-old answer to the question "where do I stand?"

The **[Swan with Two Necks](venues/swan-with-two-necks-stockport.md)** was rebuilt in **1926** after Robinsons acquired it around 1924. The archetypal **Pennine drinking lobby** has a practical logic: it provides a place to stand at the bar while smaller rooms preserve other ways to socialise. Compare its **Vault** with the **Smoke Room**, and look behind the bar for the old roller shutters protecting bottles when the pub was closed. [Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/campaigns/visit/walking-tours/historic-pub-walks-north-west-england/stockport/).

The two Robinsons houses make a **beer comparison**, not just an interiors walk. A weekday handpull might offer a drinkable house bitter such as **Unicorn (4.2%)** or a seasonal beer, but check the pump clips: a brewery catalogue is not the actual line-up. See [Robinsons' beer range](https://www.robinsonsbrewery.com/our-beers/) and our [named beers guide](what-to-drink.md).

## Old Tom: a brewery cat born on paper in 1899

Here is one unusually precise beer story: Robinsons' head brewer **Alfred "Fred" Munton wrote down Old Tom recipe no. 261 on 1 November 1899**, drawing a sketch of the brewery cat beside it. The dark strong ale, still made by the brewery, is **8.5% ABV**; its own tasting notes suggest **molasses, vinous fruit, warmth and pepper**. It is a fascinating counterpoint to a 3.8–4.2% lunchtime bitter: take a **half**, when appropriate, to appreciate the flavours without losing the afternoon. [Robinsons' history](https://www.robinsonsbrewery.com/our-beers/about-old-tom/old-tom-history/) · [Old Tom tasting notes](https://www.robinsonsbrewery.com/our-beers/old-tom/).

**Especially relevant to your dates:** Robinsons announced **Old Tom Day and its return to cask on Saturday 7 November 2026**, explicitly naming **both Arden Arms and Swan with Two Necks** as participating pubs. That is **two days before your arrival**, so you miss the launch, but the **11 November Stockport visit is a good opportunity to ask whether any cask remains**. This is a high-quality, date-specific lead, **not a promise of availability or conditioning four days later**. The bottled beer is a separate drinking experience. [Robinsons' 2026 Old Tom Day announcement](https://www.robinsonsbrewery.com/our-beers/about-old-tom/old-tom-day/).

**A bit of Stockport context:** Robinsons traces its local start to the **Unicorn Inn**, where William Robinson became landlord in **1827** and bought the building in **1838**. Its historic brewery complex helps explain the town's dense Robinsons pub footprint. Brewing tradition is not a guarantee that every venue will stock every beer. [Robinsons' brewery history](https://www.robinsonsbrewery.com/about-us/the-robinsons-story/).

## Petersgate Tap: a betting shop turned cider destination

The **[Petersgate Tap](venues/petersgate-tap-stockport.md)** opened in **2016** in a former betting shop, a neat contrast with the centuries-old rooms above. Its current specialist CAMRA record documents **six changing cask beers**, a cider fridge, bottled real ciders and **another eight real ciders and perries in the cellar**. A pub with that range deserves more than the quick question "what's on draught?" Ask for the full list, which may include bottle or cellar selections invisible from the bar. There are locally supplied pies too. [CAMRA's updated September 2026 record](https://camra.org.uk/pubs/petersgate-tap-stockport-167121).

The pub is a **2026 Greater Manchester and North West regional CAMRA Cider Pub of the Year**. Awards are useful signals of a serious drinks programme, not proof that any named bottle is open on Wednesday 11 November. [Stockport CAMRA venue record](https://camra.org.uk/pubs/petersgate-tap-stockport-167121). If perry is unfamiliar alongside apple cider, ask for a pear-fruit example and note whether the finish is floral, lightly tannic or sharply acidic; fruit variety and fermentation matter more than the word "sweet" on its own. [Cider field guide](what-to-drink.md).

## A remarkable extra curiosity (not in the 158-venue ranked catalogue)

Historic England's Stockport walk includes the **Queen's Head (former Turner's Vaults)**, whose exceptionally old bar arrangement recalls a time when **spirits were dispensed through taps fed from a room upstairs**, a system reportedly retained until **1935**. This is a fantastic *architecture-only optional enquiry*, but it has **not** been checked as an operating real-ale/cider destination for your day and is **not an added itinerary stop**. Don't assume access or trading without fresh confirmation. [Historic England Stockport walk](https://historicengland.org.uk/campaigns/visit/walking-tours/historic-pub-walks-north-west-england/stockport/).

## Three questions worth asking on Wednesday

1. **"Is the 2026 Old Tom cask still on, and how is it drinking?"** Its launch was four days earlier, but every cask turns over differently.
2. **"May we see the Select snug?"** at Arden—only with staff's permission, and only if practical.
3. **"What isn't visible on the cider board?"** at Petersgate—ask specifically for the fridge, bottles, cellar list and any perry.
